Abstract
Extracting and correlating knowledge from heterogeneous scientiﬁc publications poses a
fundamental data engineering challenge for data-driven materials discovery. Traditional keyword
search lacks semantic depth, while generic large language models (LLMs) are prone to hallucinated
predictions. To address these limitations, we present OptoMatKG, a literature-scale data engineering
system whose core contributions are threefold: (1) a domain-speciﬁc knowledge graph construction
pipeline based on a nine-category fullworkﬂow ontology; the resulting graph comprises 2.62
million entities and 3.38 million typed relations; (2) a cascaded multi-stage retrieval pipeline
that synergistically integrates LLM-based query normalization, semantic vector indexing and
Materials Project pre-ﬁltering to enhance metric-aware candidate ranking; and (3) an evidence-
constrained auditing module that forces every output to anchor to DOI-supported reasoning
chains, thereby enforcing end-to-end hallucination prevention. Using optoelectronic inverse design
as a validation testbed, experiments verify that our entity extractor reaches a micro-averaged
F1 of 0.971. Comprehensive tests over 2000 real-world queries further demonstrate that our
system surpasses keyword-based and singlestage baselines in Recall@K and metric coverage. The
system accurately quantiﬁes multi-objective performance trade-oﬀs while ensuring fully traceable
inference. Although validated on photodetectors, the proposed solution provides a generalizable,
pluggable engineering framework readily extendable to other functional material discovery
pipelines.

1 Introduction 
The rapid proliferation of heterogeneous scientific literature has created a fundamental data engineering 
challenge: how to systematically extract, structure, and retrieve cross-referenced knowledge from millions of 
published studies. In the domain of optoelectronic materials discovery, this challenge is particularly acute. [1–4] 
Traditional research workflows rely on manual operation of computational simulation packages such as VASP[5], 
Quantum-ESPRESSO[6], and Atomic Simulation Environment (ASE)[7], alongside static crystal databases including 
the Materials Project[8] and Open Quantum Materials Database (OQMD)[9], all supplemented by time-consuming 
manual reading of experimental papers. These workflows require extensive expertise to correlate material 
compositions, fabrication processes, microstructural features, characterization techniques, and device performance 
metrics—a labor-intensive process that suffers from high personal expertise dependence, low replicability, and 
frequent omission of cross-dimensional experimental evidence.[10–12] 
Existing computational approaches to accelerating this process fall into three categories: (1) high-throughput 
density functional theory (DFT) calculations, which are computationally expensive and often fail to capture 
synthesis-related experimental conditions; (2) machine learning surrogate models trained on static crystal databases, 
which generalize poorly to novel composition spaces and provide limited traceability to original experimental

evidence; and (3) keyword-based literature retrieval systems, which fail to capture domain-specific semantic 
relationships and cannot support multi-objective trade-off queries. None of these paradigms adequately addresses 
the dual requirements of optoelectronic device design: retrieving evidence-supported candidate materials while 
providing auditable reasoning chains back to primary literature, with explicit marking of unreported metrics. 
To overcome these limitations, we propose OptoMatKG, a literature-grounded knowledge graph and semantic 
retrieval framework for traceable, evidence-constrained multi-objective screening of optoelectronic materials. The 
core contributions of this work are threefold: 
(1) We construct a full-process optoelectronic ontology and a DOI-provenanced knowledge graph spanning 
experimental synthesis, fabrication processes, characterization methods, measurement data, and device performance 
metrics. This graph enables auditable cross-link reasoning across the entire material research pipeline. 
(2) We develop a cascaded multi-stage retrieval pipeline that integrates LLM-driven query normalization, 
semantic vector indexing, Materials Project pre-filtering, and metric-aware graph supplementation. This pipeline 
improves the ranking of candidates under complex multi-objective design constraints by adhering to evidence-
supported metrics only, excluding missing values from constraint satisfaction. 
(3) We design an evidence-constrained auditing module that links retrieved numerical values to verified graph 
records and primary literature DOIs. For any unreported metric, the module outputs a missing indicator (i.e., missing 
values are not used for multi-objective constraint satisfaction), while extracting interpretable reasoning paths 
to mitigate hallucinatory quantitative claims. 
2 Related Work 
The related studies are divided into the following three main lines. 
Domain-specific material knowledge graphs. Static crystal databases mainly store crystallographic and 
thermodynamic parameters, while lacking unified structured descriptions of synthesis routes, fabrication processes, 
characterization protocols and device-level optoelectronic performances[13]. Literature-mined knowledge graphs 
such as Perovskite KG[14] and MatKG[15] generally adopt shallow ontology schemas or simple co-occurrence-based 
relational links. They cannot construct complete end-to-end chains covering the full experimental workflow, and 
few of them retain permanent DOI provenance for quantitative performance verification[16]. Consequently, existing 
material graphs fail to support traceable, fully evidence-backed reasoning spanning material composition, 
fabrication processing and device performance[17], which is essential for optoelectronic multi-objective screening. 
Domain-adapted semantic retrieval strategies. Domain-pre-trained text encoders including SciBERT[18], 
MatSciBERT[19] and MatBERT[20] outperform traditional keyword matching, yet mainstream retrieval pipelines only 
index abstracts and superficial text mentions. They return unstructured text snippets instead of structured metric-
aligned graph evidence.[21] Single-stage ranking merely optimizes overall text similarity, which severely impairs 
retrieval accuracy for narrow-band optoelectronic metrics decisive to multi-objective device screening.[22] Though 
auxiliary clustering or re-ranking modules are occasionally introduced, few frameworks adopt cascaded multi-stage

filtering that fuses database-derived candidates and graph recall results, inevitably losing optimal materials targeting 
specific performance metrics.[23] 
LLM-based literature mining and hallucination suppression. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has 
become a mainstream paradigm for literature analysis.[24–26] However, most existing systems lack standardized 
optoelectronic ontologies for cross-system performance comparison, hindering quantitative evaluation of 
performance gaps between target requirements and candidate materials.[27, 28] Besides, retrieval and ranking modules 
are rarely customized for multi-objective optoelectronic demands. Without complete evidence tracing mechanisms, 
LLMs tend to generate unsubstantiated numerical predictions lacking valid literature citations.[29] Current anti-
hallucination strategies mostly rely on isolated post-hoc validation, rather than an end-to-end audit workflow that 
binds all predicted numerical results to graph records and original DOI sources.[30,31] Moreover, graph-augmented 
LLM frameworks seldom explicitly label unreported performance metrics or generate human-readable evidence-
supported reasoning chains required for optoelectronic inverse design screening.[32] 
In summary, existing approaches exhibit three primary deficiencies: (1) knowledge graphs lack full-workflow 
coverage and DOI-provenanced traceability; (2) retrieval pipelines fail to support metric-aware multi-stage filtering; 
and (3) LLM-based systems lack evidence-constrained auditing to prevent hallucinated predictions.  
3 Methodology 
Figure 1 presents the optoelectronic knowledge graph, which stores structured KG records containing domain 
entities and typed relational links. User design queries and graph records are fused via joint feature fusion, then fed 
into a domain pre-trained MatBERT model with shared encoder weights. The model generates normalized query 
vectors for user demands and record vectors for literature materials. All record vectors pass Materials Project pre-
filtering and are stored in two offline databases: vector index for fine-grained similarity matching and semantic 
clustering index for rapid coarse pre-filtering during retrieval. In the middle-right module, cosine similarity semantic 
retrieval obtains Top-K candidate materials from the vector index assisted by clustering pre-filtering.The retrieved 
candidates are imported into the evidence-constrained audit module, which sequentially implements DOI 
provenance verification, quantitative performance gap analysis, labeling of unreported performance metrics, and 
genetic algorithm (GA) multi-objective optimization, finally outputting interpretable, hallucination-free 
optoelectronic material candidates for photodetector design.

3.2 Semantic Indexing with MatBERT 
 
Figure 4. MatBERT-based semantic indexing and candidate reranking in OptoMatKG. 
Literature-derived system profiles (DOI, material, fabrication, and performance fields) are encoded by MatBERT 
using WordPiece tokenization (maximum length 512). Token embeddings are pooled via [CLS] pooling into a fixed-
length system embedding, followed by L2 normalization. User queries are projected into the same semantic space, 
and cosine similarity is used to retrieve Top-K candidate systems. Retrieved candidates are then refined by semantic 
reranking, including DOI deduplication and diversification via maximal marginal relevance (MMR) to reduce 
redundancy, yielding semantically aligned candidate sets for downstream evidence-constrained graph auditing. 
Optional clustering for latency optimization. To reduce online retrieval latency, we optionally apply K-means 
clustering over system embeddings. During inference, cosine similarity is computed only against nearest cluster 
centroids to accelerate nearest-neighbor search.

3.4 Genetic Algorithm-guided Multi-objective Candidate Refinement 
 
Figure 5. Schematic of genetic algorithm (GA) operators in OptoMatKG: individual encoding, crossover, mutation,

To improve multi-objective trade-offs after semantic retrieval, we optionally run a continuous-variable GA. Each 
individual is encoded as a normalized gene vector over a constrained design space (composition, strain, and device-
geometry variables), then decoded into physically interpretable parameters. The design-space bounds are informed 
by validated material proposals and physics checks rather than direct fitting to DOI-audited numeric ranges. 
For material-level search, the objective vector 𝐹 is minimized using bandgap mismatch and optional losses. In 
photodetector mode, response time, dark current, and detectivity are evaluated via surrogate proxy models (order-of-
magnitude device estimates), not by directly optimizing DOI-audited experimental values. Evolution uses non-
dominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II)[33], with tournament selection, simulated binary crossover 
(SBX)[34], polynomial mutation (PM; distribution index 𝜂= 15)[35], and elitist update from 𝑃𝑘 to 𝑃𝑘+1 over 30

fabricate literature evidence; it proposes proxy-level design hypotheses that complement, rather than replace, DOI-

4 Experiments and Results  
We evaluate OptoMatKG along six complementary dimensions: (1) experimental protocol covering datasets, 
baselines and evaluation metrics; (2) dual-model entity extraction performance; (3) scale and schema coverage of 
the constructed knowledge graph; (4) retrieval ablation experiments based on 2000 design queries; (5) fine-grained 
SiC case analysis and a 1550 nm photodetector screening case, which distinguishes DOI-audited experimental 
evidence from external engineering benchmarks; (6) surrogate-assisted generative design exploration for scenarios 
where limited literature data cannot resolve multi-objective trade-offs. 
4.1 Experimental setup 
Dataset and corpus. The knowledge graph is constructed from 75,979 peer-reviewed optoelectronic papers and 
stores DOI-linked Study and System records covering synthesis routes, fabrication processes, characterization 
operations, measurement data, and device performance specifications (Section 3.1). Entity extraction performance is 
evaluated on 11 held-out manuscripts annotated with fact-level labels following the proposed domain ontology. 
Retrieval experiments adopt 2000 paraphrased requirement queries generated from standardized system-profile 
cards. An additional system-profile oracle setting is deployed to examine the model’s capacity to recover 
semantically nearest-neighbor material systems. 
Baselines. For all ablation variants, the evidence-constrained auditing module is disabled, and a unified Top-K 
retrieval budget is maintained for fair comparison (Table 1). Four pipeline configurations are defined as follows: B0 
generates LLM-paraphrased candidate profiles without graph hydration; B1 implements keyword-driven Neo4j 
retrieval equipped with graph hydration; B2 performs single-stage MatBERT embedding retrieval on system profiles 
without keyword-based supplementation; B3 corresponds to our complete cascaded multi-stage pipeline. This set of 
ablations enables separate comparisons between keyword-based graph retrieval, single-stage semantic retrieval, and 
their cascaded integration under identical experimental conditions.

Metrics. For entity extraction, we adopt micro-averaged Precision, Recall and F1 calculated at the fact level 
based on true positives (TP), false positives (FP), and false negatives (FN). Retrieval performance is quantified via 
Recall@K and mean reciprocal rank (MRR) against ground-truth (DOI, system ID) pairs, together with metric 
coverage (MCov). MCov is a binary query-level indicator, assigned as 1 if any retrieved evidence entry contains at 
least one requested target-metric field within structured records, and 0 otherwise. Notably, MCov only validates the 
existence of relevant metric fields rather than compliance with numerical performance thresholds; threshold 
verification and performance gap labeling are executed in the subsequent evidence-constrained auditing stage.

To comprehensively characterize qualitative differences in Top-K candidate shortlists beyond conventional 
retrieval metrics, we introduce three supplementary evaluation metrics: UniqueDOI@K, UniqueMaterial@K and 
intra-list distance (ILD@K). UniqueDOI@K and UniqueMaterial@K denote the counts of distinct DOIs and 
normalized material strings within the Top-K retrieved entries. As an inherent constraint, UniqueDOI@K ≤ K, while 
UniqueMaterial@K may exceed K when a single evidence entry involves multiple materials. ILD@K is defined as 
the average pairwise cosine distance between MatBERT embeddings of Top-K system profiles, which reflects the 
diversity of retrieved candidate sets. 
4.2 Entity extraction quality 
Domain researchers defined the ontology schema and manually reviewed extracted Study and System records 
throughout corpus construction. For quantitative evaluation, we adopt Qwen-Plus as an automated evaluator, which 
aligns each extracted JSON record with the original manuscripts and labels each fact as TP, FP or FN following the

Page 11

10 
predefined ontology. Eleven optoelectronic manuscripts are processed via the dual-verification pipeline, with 
DeepSeek-V4-Pro serving as the default extraction model. Aggregated evaluation yields micro-averaged Precision = 
0.990, Recall = 0.952 and F1 = 0.971 (729 TP, 7 FP, 37 FN). The Study and Characterization entities achieve an F1 
score of 1.000; Property, Performance and Structure entities obtain F1 values exceeding 0.988. By contrast, Process 
(F1 = 0.928) and Measurement (F1 = 0.943) exhibit relatively lower recall, which is mainly attributed to fragmented 
textual descriptions of fabrication procedures and compact tabular measurement data in raw literature. 
To validate the reliability of automated evaluation results, a domain expert independently examined a random 
subset containing 100 extracted facts from the 11 held-out manuscripts against the original full text, following a 
standardized checklist: entity existence, numerical values consistent with corresponding metrics within a 5% 
tolerance, and complete multi-token descriptive evidence. Under this rigorous manual protocol, 79 out of 100 
extracted facts (79%) are fully supported by source literature. The unsupported entries mostly arise from absent 
entity information in the raw text corpus or isolated numerical values without associated metric descriptors, instead 
of arbitrarily generated numerical data. 
We emphasize that the manual audit adopts stricter evidence criteria than the Qwen-Plus automated evaluator. 
The human reviewer demands complete source traceability for every extracted field, while Qwen-Plus only assesses 
whether extracted information conforms to the structural constraints of the ontology schema. Consequently, the 79% 
manual support ratio reflects factual completeness and traceability, and does not conflict with the 0.990 extraction 
precision. The F1 = 0.971 effectively quantifies the extraction performance under ontology constraints, whereas the 
21% unsupported fraction illustrates inherent dataset limitations, which are further discussed in Section 6, including 
data sparsity and incomplete text coverage in scientific publications.

4.4 Retrieval ablation 
We conduct retrieval ablation experiments on 2000 paraphrased optoelectronic design queries to quantify the 
effectiveness of different pipeline components. Under the unified evaluation protocol, the keyword-enhanced graph 
retrieval (B1) and the full cascaded pipeline (B3) achieve competitive gold-DOI retrieval performance, with

and MCov results indicate that literal keyword matching dominates exact gold-DOI retrieval under the single-gold 
evaluation protocol adopted in this work. Rather than pursuing marginal improvements in exact gold recall, the 
cascaded multi-stage design of B3 focuses on optimizing the quality and diversity of retrieved candidate pools. 
Statistical results on all 2000 query samples show that B3 produces a significantly larger volume of valid evidence

rebalances the ranking distribution: B3 weakens the over-concentration of keyword-matched systems in the Top-10 
ranking while maintaining exact gold-DOI recall accuracy. Since the OptoMatKG framework incorporates a 
downstream evidence-auditing module instead of directly adopting raw Top-K results, the expanded and diversified 
candidate pool generated by B3 provides richer structured evidence for subsequent gap analysis and factual auditing. 
Further diversity verification results validate the inherent advantages of the full pipeline (Figure 10). At K=10, 
B1 and B3 exhibit comparable performance in UniqueDOI@K, UniqueMaterial@K and intra-list distance (ILD@K). 
When expanding to K=50, B3 substantially increases the number of distinct DOIs and normalized material 
candidates while maintaining competitive embedding diversity, verifying that the cascaded semantic-keyword fusion 
mechanism effectively breaks the limitation of keyword-only retrieval and enriches candidate coverage. In contrast 
to paraphrased requirement queries, system-profile oracle queries (Figure 9b) with standardized and profile-
consistent wording fully demonstrate the superiority of the full pipeline. B3 achieves a much higher Recall@50 of 
0.991 compared with 0.647 for B1, while preserving a high metric coverage of 0.991. Overall, exact gold-DOI 
retrieval for open-ended user queries is mainly constrained by keyword matching, while the proposed multi-stage 
pipeline delivers core values in expanding diverse audit candidates and improving semantic matching accuracy for 
standardized profile queries. 
We further analyze the practical significance of the obtained Recall@10 = 0.486 result. This metric indicates that 
nearly half of the paraphrased queries fail to retrieve the exact gold-standard DOI within the Top-10 candidates 
under the single-gold evaluation setting. We treat this result as an inherent limitation of the benchmark protocol 
rather than a defect of the system. Different from conventional closed-domain question-and-answer models that 
pursue exact single-answer matching, OptoMatKG is designed as an evidence-auditable material screening system. 
Its core practical value lies in providing traceable DOI-level evidence, complete metric field coverage (MCov = 
0.998), reliable long-tail retrieval capability (Recall@50 = 0.614), and explicit unreported-metric gap labeling. Even 
if the exact gold record is missing from the Top-10 ranking, the retrieved candidates still provide valid, verifiable 
structured evidence for manual inspection, screening and iteration, which conforms to the standard workflow of 
scientific material design. 
In-depth error analysis reveals three main causes for Top-10 false-negative cases: first, semantic gaps exist 
between free-form user query paraphrases and the standardized structured fields of indexed system profiles; second, 
user queries often contain underspecified or implicit constraint information, while the gold-standard system profile 
records contain richer device and process attribute details; third, the strict single-(DOI, system) labeling protocol

5 Conclusion 
This work develops OptoMatKG, a literature-grounded knowledge graph integrated with a cascaded retrieval-
and-audit framework for evidence-constrained optoelectronic material screening. Constructed from 75,979 peer-
reviewed publications, the established knowledge graph contains 2.625 million entities and 3.384 million typed 
relational edges, forming a structured, DOI-traceable data foundation for device-centric knowledge organization. 
Integrating MatBERT semantic indexing and multi-stage graph fusion, the framework converts open-ended natural-
language design queries into standardized, auditable evidence packages. These structured outputs deliver ranked 
candidate systems, quantitative performance gap annotations, fine-grained graph attributes, and interpretable 
material-process-performance reasoning chains grounded in literature. Quantitative evaluation demonstrates that the 
entity extraction pipeline achieves a micro-averaged F1 score of 0.971 on manually annotated manuscripts. 
Evaluated on 2000 practical design queries, the keyword-graph-enhanced pipeline significantly outperforms single-
stage semantic embedding retrieval in exact DOI-level system grounding, while the high metric coverage guarantees 
field-aligned structured evidence for downstream auditing, avoiding unconstrained free-form generation. 
 omprehensive case studies further validate the framework’s practicality and ro ustness.  he thin-film SiC 
su graph analysis verifies the model’s capacity for fine-grained, multi-dimensional property association and 
complete literature provenance recording. The 1550 nm photodetector screening case demonstrates that OptoMatKG 
reliably outputs partially matched literature evidence and explicitly identifies unresolvable performance gaps, 
without generating fictitious fully compliant device records. Furthermore, surrogate-assisted genetic algorithm 
exploration and commercial datasheet benchmarks are strictly categorized as model-derived engineering hypotheses, 
which are clearly decoupled from literature-verified experimental evidence to maintain factual rigor. 
We systematically analyze the limitations of the present system. Under the strict single-gold-DOI evaluation 
protocol, the best-performing cascaded pipeline (B3) achieves comparable Top-10 recall performance to pure 
keyword graph retrieval (B1), indicating that exact gold-DOI localization remains dominated by literal keyword 
matching for paraphrased user queries. Accordingly, practical screening effectiveness should be comprehensively 
evaluated by integrating metric coverage, candidate pool diversity, oracle-query retrieval accuracy, deep Recall@K 
performance, and auditable gap labeling, rather than relying solely on top-ranked exact matching. A domain expert 
checklist-based audit on 100 randomly sampled extracted facts validates that 79% of the structured records are fully 
traceable to original full-text content. In-depth error analysis further attributes most Top-10 false-negative cases to 
deep ranking failures and near-miss gold rankings, rather than incomplete profile coverage in the knowledge base.

Despite the inherent coverage limitation of the finite literature corpus, OptoMatKG exhibits distinct advantages 
over conventional retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems. It strictly constrains all numerical performance 
reports to DOI-verified graph evidence, explicitly marks unreported metric fields, and systematically separates 
model-generated engineering hypotheses from literature-supported factual claims. Different from traditional open-
ended generation paradigms, this framework prioritizes traceability, auditability and factual reliability for scientific 
material screening. Future directions include enhancing query normalization and paraphrase adaptation, improving 
top-rank candidate diversity, expanding evolutionary optimization budgets to achieve statistically significant 
generative exploration, strengthening cross-study relational reasoning, and releasing a public query API to facilitate 
open-source data science applications.
